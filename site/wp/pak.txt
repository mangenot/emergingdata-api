[ed_overview date="17 November 2026"]
[ed_standfirst]Pakistan reaches the end of 2026 as the mediator of the war in the Gulf and one of its casualties: diesel is a third dearer than a year ago, inflation ran above 10% for six months before easing to 9.6% in October, and the State Bank's rate is back at 12%. The macro repair holds: reserves of USD 31.6 billion including gold, a rupee 1.5% stronger on the year at 276.6, remittances at a record USD 3.9 billion in October, and the IMF's fourth review before the board in December. Exports grow 8.5% and textiles 5%, into an EU that is duty-free and a United States that charges 26%; the Afghan and Iranian borders are closed, the insurgency has had its worst year in a decade, and the wage is USD 147 at the federal scale and USD 155 in Sindh.[/ed_standfirst]
<p>On 13 November the government cut petrol to Rs 332 a litre and diesel to Rs 360, the sixth fortnightly reduction since the April peak of Rs 458 and Rs 520, and still 25% and 31% above a year earlier. The same week the State Bank reported remittances of USD 3.9 billion for October, 14% more than a year earlier and USD 14.8 billion in four months, enough to cover most of a trade deficit that reached USD 14.6 billion in July to October, and liquid reserves of USD 21.6 billion of its own, USD 26.8 billion with the banks.</p>
<p>The IMF board is expected in the second week of December to release USD 1.2 billion, the fourth review of the Extended Fund Facility and the third of the climate facility, after the staff agreement of 8 October; USD 5.7 billion of the USD 7 billion programme will then have been drawn. The Monetary Policy Committee meets in mid-December with inflation at 9.6% in October, down from 10.3%, and the rate at 12% since 27 October. On 12 November the power regulator notified a special rate of Rs 25.30 per unit for industry's additional consumption, over the dissent of two of its members, against a normal industrial bill of about Rs 34.50.</p>
<p>Behind the figures the security year has been the worst in a decade: the Quetta train bombing of 24 May with 30 dead, the attack on the Rangers' headquarters in Karachi on 27 June, 42 dead in Balochistan in the first week of July, 27 in Tank on 24 July, 31 in Kohat on 18 September. The border with Afghanistan has been closed to trade since October 2025 and Pakistan struck eastern Afghanistan on 28 June; with India shut since 2019 and Iran shut by the war, the only open land route is the road to China.</p>
<h3>The latest data</h3>
<p>Inflation, 5.8% in January, went to 10.9% in April and 11.7% in May with the fuel shock of the war in the Gulf, and has come down to 9.6% in October, with transport still at 28.6%, housing and utilities at 11.0%, food at 7.7% and core at 7.5%; the target is 5 to 7%. The State Bank, which had cut from 22% to 10.5% between June 2024 and December 2025, raised to 11.5% in April and to 12% on 27 October by nine votes to one; the lending rate proxy of 16% is the dearest of the five countries covered. Private credit grows 15% a year all the same, and non-performing loans are 5.5%, the cleanest book of the five.</p>
<p>Exports were USD 11.4 billion in July to October, up 8.5%, and imports USD 26.0 billion, up 12.5%, the oil bill up 8% in value on lower volumes; October exports of USD 2.9 billion were 2.8% above a year earlier. Textiles and clothing made USD 5.03 billion in the first quarter, up 5.3%, with readymade garments up 16%, knitwear up 4% and bedwear flat; the apparel series stood at USD 875 million in September, 8% above a year earlier and a record for the month. Cotton arrivals were 27% ahead of last year at the end of August, the crop has passed 4 million bales, and imports from the United States and Brazil continue.</p>
<p>Reserves including gold were USD 31.6 billion at the end of September on the IMF definition, 35% above a year earlier; the State Bank's liquid holdings were USD 21.6 billion on 6 November and the commercial banks' USD 5.3 billion. The rupee at 276.6 is 1.5% stronger than a year ago. Remittances of USD 42.9 billion over the twelve months to September, and USD 14.8 billion in the four months of the fiscal year, are the counterweight to a current account that the oil bill has pushed back into deficit.</p>
<h3>The fuel bill</h3>
<p>Pakistan imports its oil and a third of its gas. The war in the Gulf, which began on 28 February with the American and Israeli strikes on Iran, took petrol from Rs 265 to Rs 458 and diesel from Rs 275 to Rs 520 by 3 April, brought a four-day week for government offices and a school closure in March, and naval escorts for tankers into Karachi. The Islamabad memorandum of June paused the fighting and collapsed within weeks over the Strait of Hormuz; the American blockade of Iranian ports has been in place since 14 July, the strait is restricted, and the talks are stalled, with Pakistan and Qatar as mediators.</p>
<p>For the mills the bill arrives three ways: diesel for transport at Rs 360; the fuel charge adjustment on power bills, which the regulator spreads over all consumers; and gas for the captive plants of the spinning and processing industry at about USD 13.7 per MMBtu, after the April agreement with the IMF that cut the captive power levy from Rs 1,303 to about Rs 522. The base rate for a B-3 industrial connection is Rs 27 per unit since February, about 9.7 US cents, and the bill about Rs 34.50 with the adjustments, 12.5 cents; the special rate of Rs 25.30 for additional consumption, notified on 12 November, replaces the Rs 22.98 of last winter.</p>
<h3>The security year</h3>
<p>The Tehreek-e-Taliban and the Baloch insurgency have made 2026 the most violent year since the mid-2010s: the Quetta train bombing of 24 May, claimed by the BLA; the Rangers' headquarters in Karachi on 27 June; 42 dead in Balochistan between 4 and 8 July; 27 in Tank on 24 July; 14 at an anti-militant rally in Swat on 2 August; 31 at a police facility in Kohat on 18 and 19 September. Pakistan struck eastern Afghanistan on 28 June, and the border has been closed to trade since October 2025, a reopening decided in August not having been carried out. For the industry in Karachi, Faisalabad and Lahore the violence is at the periphery, in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan, with Karachi touched twice: the riot at the US consulate on 1 March, at the start of the war, left ten dead.</p>
<p>The political order was settled by the 27th amendment of November 2025: Field Marshal Asim Munir as Chief of Defence Forces with command of the three services and the nuclear command, a Federal Constitutional Court above the Supreme Court, and a prime minister whose defence role is ceremonial. Imran Khan has been in Adiala jail since August 2023; the Supreme Court's order of 18 August to move him to hospital was taken up by the constitutional court in September. The coalition under Shehbaz Sharif runs to early 2029. Protest events on GDELT run at 500 to 700 a month, 533 in October, 0.8 times the yearly average; the peaks of 2023, 2,251 in the month of Khan's arrest, have not returned.</p>
<h3>The IMF anchor</h3>
<p>The USD 7 billion Extended Fund Facility of September 2024 runs to late 2027 and sets the calendar: the staff agreement on the fourth review came on 8 October with the primary surplus kept for 2026/27, the board in December releases USD 1.0 billion plus USD 210 million from the climate facility, and the fifth review mission comes in the spring. Public debt is 73% of GDP (fiscal 2025), the deficit 5.4%, external debt service 40% of exports in 2024. The agencies moved up in 2026: S&P to B on 21 July, Moody's to B3 on 24 August, Fitch B- affirmed in April.</p>
<p>What the programme buys the buyer is predictability: no import restrictions, letters of credit opening normally since 2023, a rupee that the central bank keeps in a narrow band and that has strengthened for two years, and an energy price policy negotiated with the Fund, which means that the captive power levy, the industrial tariff packages and the fuel levy all pass through Washington before they reach the mills.</p>
<h3>Market access</h3>
<p>The EU takes about 35% of apparel exports duty-free under GSP+; Regulation (EU) 2026/1395 applies from 1 January 2027 with the preferences continuing, and a reapplication with an action plan on 32 conventions is due by the end of 2028. The United States takes about 22% at a duty of about 26%, the 16% MFN rate plus the 10% Section 301 forced-labour duty of 24 July, with no textile tariff-rate quota; the reciprocal trade framework negotiated since July, with significant progress reported by both sides on 5 August, has no text. The United Kingdom applies the enhanced DCTS preferences. The Afghan and Gulf markets, small for apparel but large for other textiles, are the ones the border closure and the war have cost.</p>
<h3>Forty years of textiles</h3>
<p>Textiles are more than half of exports and the largest industrial employer, from cotton growing through spinning, the country's strength, to garments, where it is smaller than Bangladesh: knitwear and readymade garments bring about USD 9 billion a year, bedwear and towels about USD 4.5 billion, yarn and cloth the rest. The integrated mill, which spins its own yarn and runs its own gas-fired power plant, is the typical supplier, which is why gas and power prices matter more here than anywhere else in the set, and why the wage, USD 147 at the federal scale of Rs 40,700 and USD 155 at Sindh's Rs 43,000, in the middle of the five countries covered, is a smaller part of the cost. Inflation above 10% for six months, after 38% in 2023, erodes a scale that is reset only with the June budgets.</p>
<p>The rupee is the other structural fact: 160 to the dollar in 2021, 298 in 2023, 276.6 today, held by the central bank's purchases and by remittances. Two years of slow appreciation with 10% inflation have raised dollar costs by about a tenth, the largest real appreciation of the five.</p>
<h3>What it means for buyers</h3>
<p>The costs: a wage of USD 147 to 155 reset in June, power at about 12.5 US cents on the bill and gas at 13.7 dollars, diesel a third dearer than a year ago, a duty of 26% into the United States and zero into the EU. The calendar: the IMF board and the rate decision in December, the fifth review in the spring, the budgets in June with the wage, the Eid closures of March and May, the monsoon from July. The risks: the Strait of Hormuz, which sets the fuel price and the shipping route from Karachi; the Afghan border and the attacks in the north-west and Balochistan, which do not reach the mills but reach the roads; the GSP+ file in Brussels.</p>
<p>Three checks at supplier level: the energy mix of the mill, grid, captive gas or furnace oil, and its exposure to the levy and the adjustments; the port routing while Hormuz is restricted; and the provincial wage notification that applies, Sindh standing above the federal scale.</p>

[ed_lead]The best connected of the five countries covered, with mainline calls at Karachi and Port Qasim (3.3 million TEU) and the Strait of Hormuz a day's sailing away: war-risk premiums and naval escorts since March, container delays at the ports in August and September. [ed_text]
Pakistan's apparel leaves through Karachi and Port Qasim, fed by the Punjab textile belt around Faisalabad and Lahore by road and rail.
Container traffic through the two ports was 3.34 million TEU in 2024, 15% more than in 2023 (World Bank), and the liner shipping connectivity index of 34 (2021) is more than twice Kenya's and four times Cambodia's.
The last Logistics Performance Index survey to include Pakistan (2018) scored it 2.4 out of 5 and 2.1 on customs, the lowest customs score of the five.
The war in the Gulf has made the Arabian Sea a war-risk zone: the navy has escorted tankers into Karachi since 9 March, the Strait of Hormuz is restricted with attacks on shipping at more than one a day, and premiums apply to calls at Karachi; the mills' association reported hundreds of containers of imported inputs and export consignments delayed at the ports in August and September.
The Drewry World Container Index was USD 4,305 per 40ft on 12 November, down 2% on the week, with Shanghai to Rotterdam at 3,504, Shanghai to Los Angeles at 7,416 and Shanghai to New York at 9,020; Drewry expects transpacific rates to fall further and Asia to Europe to firm.
The monsoon floods of 2022 and 2025 hit cotton and transport in Punjab and Sindh; the two Eid closures of March and May bunch shipments.
